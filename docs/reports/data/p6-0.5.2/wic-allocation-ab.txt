The VirtualQuery probe compiled with /W4 /WX /analyze in x64 and x86 and reported
exact +262,144 and -262,144 B deltas across a controlled VirtualAlloc/VirtualFree.
Fixed sampler commit was 69,632 B (x64) and 36,864 B (x86), reported separately.
The heap walk is bounded to 65,536 default-process-heap entries while locked.
The process PrivateUsage counters include sampler commit. MEM_PRIVATE sums exclude
the diagnostic workspace and do not include copy-on-write MEM_IMAGE/MEM_MAPPED.

DecodeProbe.exe ran the unmodified production reader and canvas over 100 cycles,
alternating two saved VisPick recordings. Post-decode PrivateUsage from cycle 4
ranged 6,336,512–8,364,032 B and ended at 7,139,328 B. Default heap busy payload
ranged 454,786–456,626 B, free payload 2,534,960–3,049,696 B, and enumerated
region commit 3,182,592–3,727,360 B. This isolates a large allocator capacity
effect without comparable persistent live allocation growth.

DecodeReuseProbe.exe compared fresh per-frame vectors against one reused frame
vector per decode cycle using a scratch copy of the production reader.
The scratch overload resets metadata each frame. Both modes use one Release
x64 executable, a 20 ms inter-cycle delay, same decode/composition and same
sampled pixel/timing checksum. No desktop, product source edits, heap trims or
COM-lifetime changes. Each fixture ran three pairs of fresh/reuse in alternating
order, 100 cycles per process with cycles 1–3 warmup and cycle 3 as baseline.

for either fixture. The recorded fixture had lower median commit with reuse,
while peak growth remained above 5% in every run. Variation from the
allocator is large relative to the measured effect of the C++ frame vector.
Retain the production API until there is independent justification for a change.
